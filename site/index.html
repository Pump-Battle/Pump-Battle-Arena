    Rules

    How a match works

    1. One coin, one matchWe launch a new coin on pump.fun. In the same transaction the program buys the creator's share and locks it. The program checks that nobody bought before it did.
    2. The pot comes from feesThe program is the coin's creator on pump.fun, so it gets every creator fee the coin earns. A quarter goes into the pot. The rest goes to the creator.
    3. The lock ends at a set timeThen the program sells the whole bag. The creator gets back what it cost. Half of any profit goes into the pot. Right after the lock ends only our keeper can sell, with a price floor, so nobody can front-run it.
    4. The bell is randomAfter the lock ends, anyone can ring the bell. It picks a random moment in the last ten minutes of the match. Only trades before that moment count. Nobody knows the moment until it is picked.
    5. Score is the profit you tookWhat you got from your sells minus what those tokens cost you, straight from the chain. Tokens you still hold count for nothing. If you never sold, you are not ranked.
    6. Anyone can check the winnerThe scorer posts the winner and a hash of the full table. Anyone can redo the math from public data and compare. After a short wait, anyone can trigger the payout.
    7. Nobody can get stuckIf no result is posted in time, the pot goes to a backup address set at launch. Once the pot is paid, anyone can close the match and the creator gets the rent back.

    How to read a match

    Each match is one account on the chain (376 bytes). The fields, in order:

    cost_basis
    what the program paid for the bag
    tokens_locked
    the bag
    unlock_ts
    when the lock ends
    window
    the earliest and latest second the bell can ring
    fee_total / fee_prize
    fees collected, and the part in the pot
    proceeds / gain_prize
    what the bag sold for, and the profit share in the pot
    prize_pot
    what the winner gets
    candle_end
    when the bell rang
    winner / results_hash
    the winner and the table hash
    deadline
    when the payout can happen

    The site reads this live. The reader is in the code.

    Fighters · idea for $FIGHT

    Every wallet gets a fighter

    Your fighter comes from your wallet address: a type, a look, gear and a name. No two wallets look alike. The idea: an arena token, $FIGHT, that lets you mint a fighter with gear you pick. Fighters are just for looks. The score is always real profit on the chain.

    Before we make a token: a token that only buys looks is the least risky kind, but it is still a token we issue. We write the rules down and check them before anyone buys one.
